Training

GPS distance & watch duplicates

Filter small GPS movements while standing still, and keep one activity for an overlapping phone and watch recording.

How to use it

Distance ignores fixes worse than about ±25 m and movement smaller than the GPS error (at least 5 m); GPS is still approximate. When at least 80% of the longer workout overlaps, a phone recording and imported watch workout merge in either order, with available watch heart rate. A short run inside a long hike stays separate, as do two phone recordings.

Progress

Fitness, fatigue and form defined

Fitness follows longer-term training load; fatigue follows recent load. Form is fitness minus fatigue.

How to use it

Fitness and fatigue use smoothed load over about 42 and 7 days. Positive form means recent load is below your longer-term load; negative form means it is above. These numbers do not measure how you feel or diagnose overtraining. Missing heart rate can limit the load estimate.

Progress

VDOT and race predictions

VDOT is a running-performance score calculated from distance and time, not a measured oxygen test.

How to use it

The current score uses your best recorded efforts of a mile or longer in the last 90 days. Predictions use a source effort for each distance and recent running mileage. A fast short effort may still overstate marathon ability. Course, weather, pacing and endurance matter; these are estimates, not guaranteed finish times.

Training

Pace, GAP and effort

Pace is time per mile or kilometre. GAP estimates a comparable pace on level ground. Effort estimates training load from duration and heart rate.

How to use it

Inspect an activity’s metrics and splits. GAP is a model of slope effects, not a measured flat-course result. Effort depends on available heart rate and your settings; missing values stay unavailable. Heart-rate drift compares the relationship between pace and heart rate across halves; heat, hills and stops can affect it.
