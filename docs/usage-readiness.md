# Usability and readiness for more users

Review: 20 October 2026. This is an engineering assessment, not approval to expand the live service. Code review, merge, server deployment, signed iPhone upload, enabling Kit and physical-device acceptance are separate steps. See [beta readiness](beta-readiness.md), [the roadmap](../ROADMAP.md) and [Kit's decision record](kit-assistant.md).

## Changes in this PR

- New accounts start with Everyone on Kinetta for new activities. Signup lets the person choose Everyone, approved friends or Only me before any import. Existing accounts keep their saved default, because old default values cannot reliably be distinguished from deliberate privacy choices. Existing activities are not republished. Phone recordings follow the default and show an editable audience before Save; retry does not change an already-saved recording. Failed sharing-setting saves restore the actual saved selection.
- Read-only tab homes offer pull-to-refresh at the top plus a labelled Refresh page button. Cached content remains available during the request and after temporary failure. Horizontal/map gestures, editors, selection, open settings, chats and recording retain their behavior. No Health sync or write is initiated by refresh.
- Returning to the app no longer rebuilds an active chat or form after a minute. Loading cannot move the page after the user starts interacting. Ordinary GET requests have a bounded wait. Existing cached screens, deduplicated reads, pagination, lazy maps and stable tab navigation remain in use.
- Me explains “Ask Kit — Your training & nutrition assistant” with a direct invitation. Each Kit visit starts fresh; unsent/unanswered text survives as a draft without automatic resend. Other devices keep independent chats. Up to twelve exact short statements about preferences/goals/routines can carry context forward, with expiry and view/delete/off controls. The conservative selector does not understand or retain every important detail. Current user wording and current tools take priority.

## Speed evidence and limits

On the isolated fictional install, the initial baseline's ordinary tab ready callbacks took approximately 16–84 ms on localhost. This does not measure a real user's network, public proxy, older phone, initial download, map tiles or a large history. The problem observed here was disruptive rebuilding and delayed scroll restoration, rather than evidence that a database rewrite is needed.

Validate warm navigation, refresh with a delayed response, failure with cached data and foreground recovery as separate flows. Record user-visible first content separately from final network completion. Use comparable data, device and network conditions before claiming improvement. Local small-fixture concurrency checks are smoke evidence, never a supported-user count.

Use [Google's responsiveness target](https://web.dev/articles/inp) of 200 ms or less for INP, and [its loading guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/core-web-vitals) of 2.5 seconds or less for LCP, at the 75th percentile. These are measurement targets, not results for Kinetta. [Apple's loading guidance](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/loading) supports immediate content and understandable progress. Review status feedback and visible retained content on actual iPhones with slow/offline connections.

## Before inviting the next five testers

1. **Verify a single release.** Record the deployed server revision and processed TestFlight build. On physical iPhones check signup/sharing, Health consent/history/new workouts, background/locked-screen recording, keyboard and VoiceOver, refresh, offline food/recording retries, logout and account switching. Browser checks and unsigned builds do not close these items.
2. **Review Kit's changed context.** Its new memory behavior changes the release fingerprint. Compare real responses across visits, changed/conflicting preferences, forgetting/off, malicious saved wording and Health revocation against the reviewed version, then obtain qualitative owner review. Supply a matching private report only after that review; previous answers and synthetic tests are not approval. Existing food estimation/understanding gates remain separate. No paid model evaluation is run for this PR.
3. **Close the security and recovery gaps.** Follow up the operator's separate private security review. Verify trusted-proxy HTTPS/Secure cookies, authenticated ownership and least privilege, limits on login/uploads/expensive endpoints, current host/container patches and isolation from unrelated services. Verify encrypted off-site copies, retention and an isolated restore of database plus uploaded files; document the recovery time and rollback. Do not publish deployment details or real records in this public repository.
4. **Monitor failures and cost.** Configure health/uptime checks, actionable error alerts, sync/import failures, storage/backup age and AI cost/concurrency caps. Use aggregate timings and counts rather than conversation/Health payloads. Verify alert delivery and a support contact; establish an owner for triage.
5. **Make the first-run path self-service.** Test invite → signup with an explicit audience → TestFlight → Health/phone recording → first activity and food entry. Give testers one feedback route and recoverable error instructions. Confirm Apple review/install status, privacy metadata and the intended age/audience/provider policies before widening access.

## Before expanding beyond the small beta

Run the existing five-person, two-week acceptance study: food logged most days, workouts arrive or are recorded without developer help, and no unresolved data-loss/privacy defects. Then load-test a disposable copy with realistic fictional long histories and a mix of food, feeds, progress, uploads and imports. Measure median/p95/p99 request latency, errors, worker/DB-pool queueing, CPU/memory/storage, map/food-provider quotas and AI spend at increasing concurrency. Include account isolation, slow providers, simultaneous imports, exhausted budgets and recovery after failure. Choose the next audience limit from those results and the support burden; do not infer hundreds of users from a passing functional suite or add infrastructure speculatively.

Suggested engineering targets for owner review: zero failed requests in the planned beta load, p95 ordinary reads below 500 ms at the app boundary, visible response to a tap within 200 ms, bounded expensive jobs with status/retry and no duplicated writes. Public network latency and full-screen loading need their own measurements. These thresholds are proposed acceptance criteria, not an availability promise.
